Buchwald–Hartwig tasks use the Ahneman, Estrada, Lin, Dreher and Doyle high-throughput C–N coupling screen (Science 2018, 360, 186–190, DOI 10.1126/science.aar5169). The table is sheet `FullCV_01` of `Dreher_and_Doyle_input_data.xlsx` from [rxn4chemistry/rxn_yields](https://github.com/rxn4chemistry/rxn_yields/blob/master/data/Buchwald-Hartwig/Dreher_and_Doyle_input_data.xlsx). That sheet has 3,955 rows and the columns Ligand, Additive, Base, Aryl halide and Output. The other sheets in the workbook are reshuffles of the same screen and were not added. Output is already a yield percentage (minimum 0, maximum 99.99999), so yields were not multiplied by 100. No condition was repeated, so averaging duplicate rows did not change any value. The sheet contains 15 aryl halides, 4 ligands, 3 bases and 22 isoxazole additives. A full 15 × 4 × 3 × 22 grid would be 3,960 reactions; five combinations are absent. The coupling partner is 4-methylaniline (p-toluidine): Ahneman et al. use that name, and the rxn_yields reaction builder hard-codes SMILES `Cc1ccc(N)cc1`.

Ligand and base names were read off the SMILES, not taken from a name column in the file. The ligands are tBuBrettPhos, tBuXPhos, XPhos and AdBrettPhos. The bases are BTMG, P2Et and MTBD. The 22 additives have short names parsed from the SMILES (for example 3,5-dimethylisoxazole, 5-phenylisoxazole, 1,2-benzisoxazole). Labels `L1`…, `B1`… and `A1`… are the raw SMILES in alphabetical order and are the same in every BH task (`options_BH.json`). `names.csv` maps each label to its name and SMILES.

A task fixes the aryl halide and varies ligand, base and additive. Any aryl halide whose best yield is under 30% is dropped: that removes 1-chloro-4-ethylbenzene (262 conditions, maximum yield 16.2490011). Five tasks were kept so the maximum yields span the screen and so chloride, bromide and iodide all appear. Ordered by increasing maximum yield:

BH_t2 has 263 measured rows rather than 264 because one combination is missing from the source sheet. For these sizes, k = max(3, ceil(0.01 × n)) = 3, so the hit threshold is the third-highest yield. Each BH task has exactly three hits.

Direct arylation is included as one task, `DA`. It is reaction 3 of Shields et al., the palladium-catalysed imidazole C–H arylation related to a step toward BMS-911543 (Nature 2021, 590, 89–96, DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-03213-y). The table is `experiments/data/direct_arylation/experiment_index.csv` in [b-shields/edbo](https://github.com/b-shields/edbo/blob/master/experiments/data/direct_arylation/experiment_index.csv), with names from that folder’s ligand, base and solvent lists. It has 1,728 rows, no duplicate conditions and no empty cells: 4 bases × 12 ligands × 4 solvents × 3 concentrations (0.057, 0.1 and 0.153 M) × 3 temperatures (90, 105 and 120 °C). Yields are already on a 0–100 scale (minimum 0, maximum 100) and were not rescaled. Concentration and temperature are categories. Ligand names are the EDBO list names (BrettPhos, X-Phos, JackiePhos, CgMe-PPh, PCy3 HBF4, and the rest). Bases are potassium or cesium acetate or pivalate. Solvents are N,N-dimethylacetamide, p-xylene, butyronitrile and n-butyl acetate. The reaction text names the product as 5-(2-fluorophenyl)-1-methyl-1H-imidazole-4-carbonitrile, which is the product described for this benchmark; the EDBO index has no substrate SMILES column. The budget is 30. Here n = 1,728, so k = ceil(17.28) = 18 and the hit threshold is the 18th-highest yield, 90.39. There are 18 hits. `DA` is the only task in its dataset, so `get_transfer_data("DA")` returns empty `X` and `y` with width 27 (26 one-hot columns plus one substrate column).

A hit, in every dataset, is defined from the measured yields of that task. Sort the yields descending and set k = max(3, ceil(0.01 × n_rows)). The hit threshold is the k-th highest yield, counting from 1. A condition is a hit when its yield is greater than or equal to that threshold. Yields are percentages on a 0–100 scale.

`task.X` is a one-hot matrix from `src/encode.py`. Columns follow the global option order in `options_BH.json` or `options_DA.json`: for each variable, one column per option. BH tasks share 29 columns (4 ligands + 3 bases + 22 additives). The DA task has 26 columns (4 bases + 12 ligands + 4 solvents + 3 concentrations + 3 temperatures). The substrate block is not inside `task.X`. `get_transfer_data` and `pad_with_substrate_block` append it. For BH that block has five columns, in sorted task-id order `BH_t1`…`BH_t5`. Historical rows from the other substrates use their own column.

Suzuki–Miyaura was not built. There was not enough time left after BH and DA to clean and name that screen.
